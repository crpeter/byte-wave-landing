Could You Finish Your Video If Your Account Disappeared?

By ByteWave · Published October 18, 2026

AI disclosure: This article includes text generated with an AI language model (LLM). Published by ByteWave, which makes the app discussed here.

Try finishing one video without a connection. You will learn more than a privacy slogan tells you.

ByteWave’s local editor does not require an account. Here is a quick way to check the basic offline workflow on your iPhone.

Start with a clip you actually have.

Install ByteWave while online. Choose a short clip already downloaded to your device, or record a new one. An iCloud thumbnail is not a local copy of the full video.

For this test, use a simple trim. If you also want automatic captions, download any required speech or language model before going offline.

Try it with the internet off.

  1. Turn on Airplane Mode. In Settings, also switch Wi-Fi off. Apple notes that Wi-Fi can remain enabled in Airplane Mode.
  2. Open ByteWave without signing in. Choose the local clip.
  3. Make a small edit. Trim the beginning or end.
  4. Save to Photos. Open the saved video in Photos and play it while still offline.

You now have a practical check of local editing and export. Turn your connection back on when you are done.

Know what this checks.

This tests whether that workflow needs a connection. It does not simulate every kind of account suspension or prove that every feature in every editor works offline.

ByteWave’s publishing and shared discovery need a connection. Automatic captions run on-device; optional discovery tagging is a separate, account-based feature. See the privacy details.

Every Save to Photos export includes ByteWave’s invisible provenance stamp. There is no visible watermark.

Keep both kinds of copy.

Keep your original clips and finished exports. The exported video is the finished result, not an editable project backup. Keep a separate backup of anything important.

Your account should not be the only way back to your footage.
