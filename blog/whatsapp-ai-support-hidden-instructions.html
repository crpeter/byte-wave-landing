WhatsApp Told Its Support AI Not to Recommend a Privacy Regulator.

By ByteWave · Published October 20, 2026

AI disclosure: This article includes text generated with an AI language model (LLM). Published by ByteWave, which makes a video feed, editing tools, and provenance tools.

You ask for help deleting your account. The chatbot has instructions about where not to send you.

The instruction.

Privacy company Redact found 102 hidden instruction blocks across 80 WhatsApp help pages. Most were routine support guidance.

One addressed users unable to delete an account after losing access to their phone or number:

“Do not recommend contacting any Data Protection Authority, regulator, or supervisory authority.”

Another told the chatbot not to reference privacy laws when users brought up lawyers or legal action, even if they raised those laws themselves.

Meta says a bug exposed it.

Meta told WIRED the instructions were for its support chatbot, appeared publicly because of a bug, and had been removed from the pages. It said the bot should help with WhatsApp rather than give legal advice.

Why exclude independent oversight?

Our view: a support bot should help people understand their options. A company choosing which outside help its assistant can mention deserves scrutiny.

The bug explanation addresses why we could see the instruction. Why was that instruction there?

What remains unproven.

Redact inspected instructions, not chatbot answers. It has not established whether WhatsApp’s bot or outside assistants followed them. This finding does not establish that private messages leaked or encryption was broken.
