No AI Label Doesn’t Mean the Video Is Real.

By ByteWave · Published September 22, 2026

AI disclosure: This article includes text generated with an AI language model (LLM). Published by ByteWave, which makes the app discussed here.

You see a convincing video. Facebook shows no AI warning. That is not a reason to trust it.

Facebook left this one unlabeled.

On September 17, 2026, Meta’s Oversight Board ordered the removal of a deepfake depicting a Scottish councillor because it violated hate-speech rules. Meta had left it online and decided it did not merit an AI label. The Board disagreed and found shortcomings in Meta’s labeling policies. Read the Board’s findings.

An absent warning tells you no warning is displayed. It does not establish who made the video or whether the scene happened.

Check the evidence behind the clip.

Content Credentials can carry signed information about a file’s origin and edits, including a declaration of AI generation. They are optional and can be lost. Missing credentials do not prove a file is real—or fake. Valid credentials do not prove the story attached to it. C2PA explains these limits.

ByteWave can inspect supported embedded credentials. Follow our C2PA checking tutorial to understand the result. An inconclusive result should stay inconclusive.

Before sharing, find the original source and check the date and context. Ask for the original file when a forwarded copy has lost useful information.

You do not owe an unverified video another view, repost, or argument.
