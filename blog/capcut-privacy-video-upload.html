Opening a local CapCut project does not justify the blanket claim that every clip is immediately uploaded. CapCut does offer cloud storage, syncing, publishing, and AI features that can send content to its services. The practical privacy question is which features you use and what the current policy authorizes.

For many casual users, this isn't a concern. But for creators working with sensitive material, businesses creating confidential content, or anyone who simply prefers to keep their personal videos private, it's worth understanding what's happening behind the scenes.

What gets uploaded?

When you use cloud-dependent features—including cloud storage, cross-device syncing, publishing, and certain AI tools—content must leave the device to provide that service. Review CapCut's current privacy policy for the categories it says it processes and the purposes it lists; product behavior and policies can change.

The on-device alternative

ByteWave separates editing from online participation. Its editing and rendering pipeline runs locally on supported Apple devices, and no account is required. Footage reaches ByteWave's online services only when you deliberately publish or share it through an online feature. Automatic video tagging is separate and requires an account and opt-in; see the privacy policy.

This on-device approach has performance benefits too. There's no upload/download wait time. You can edit without an internet connection. And export speeds depend on your hardware, not server load.

What should you consider?

If you're editing personal family videos, client work under NDA, pre-release content, or anything you'd rather not have sitting on a third-party server, consider whether cloud-based processing aligns with your comfort level. On-device editors like ByteWave offer the same creative capabilities without the privacy tradeoff.
