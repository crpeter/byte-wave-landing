Our view: $25 billion is nowhere near enough. This judgment concerns Facebook’s deception about privacy. Our criticism goes further, including the harm to children at the center of separate cases. We don’t believe this penalty comes close to accounting for the damage Meta has done.

What the court ordered.

On October 21, 2026, a New Mexico judge ordered Meta to pay $25 billion following a jury finding of more than 43 million consumer-protection violations. The case grew out of Cambridge Analytica’s access to Facebook user data.

Meta plans to appeal. Its lawyers dispute the evidence of harm and argue that an excessive penalty would violate due process. This is an order to pay, not money already collected.

Children’s harm deserves its own accounting.

The $25 billion ruling is not a child-safety damages award. In separate litigation, state attorneys general alleged Meta knowingly used harmful, addictive features to keep children engaged. That settlement required payments and changes to youth protections; it did not establish every allegation as fact.

There is a connection between the settlements: the attorneys general’s database identifies a separate $459.3 million Cambridge Analytica payment within the broader 2026 agreement. That payment is distinct from the youth-safety payments and today’s New Mexico judgment.

A penalty cannot undo the damage.

Our opinion: accountability must mean more than a large number in a headline. It must mean fewer people harmed, stronger privacy, and changes that last.
