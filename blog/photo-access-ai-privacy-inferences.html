You agreed to share a file. Did you agree to the profile someone could build from it?

A September 22, 2026 research preprint examines what AI can infer from smartphone data. Researchers built Priva-See and ran a study with 465 participants. They reported privacy-invasive inferences even with access to only part of each person’s data.

The study covers smartphone information including photos and other permission-controlled data. It is not proof that Meta, or any particular app, runs this system.

The file is only the beginning.

A permission prompt can say “photos.” That does not explain every conclusion a system might draw from them, or whether those conclusions are right.

A wrong profile can still be an intrusive profile.

This is why “you gave permission” is an incomplete answer to privacy concerns. People need to understand what happens after access is granted.

Give less access. Keep more local.

Share only the files a task needs. Review photo permissions for apps you no longer use. Choose tools that can do the work on your device.

ByteWave lets you edit locally without uploading your footage. If you later upload the result elsewhere, that service gets the content you share.

Removing metadata can reduce hidden information. It cannot hide what is visible or audible in the file. See what photos and videos can reveal.
