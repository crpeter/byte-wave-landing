The company deciding what gets seen may be the company processing the payment.

itch.io showed how that works.

On July 24, 2025, itch.io said it had removed all adult NSFW content from its browse and search pages after scrutiny from payment processors. The platform said protecting those payment relationships was essential to its wider marketplace.

Deindexing was not the same as deleting every game. Pages and existing purchases could remain accessible while discovery disappeared. For a small creator, losing discovery still matters.

The pressure reaches beyond the storefront.

On July 31, itch.io announced it was reindexing free adult content while negotiating how to restore paid content. It said Stripe cited restrictions from its banking partners, even though card networks generally support adult content with appropriate registration.

Those distinctions matter. Banks, processors, card networks, and platforms each have different roles. This was not evidence that every affected work was illegal, or that one card company directly deleted every title.

Our concern is broader: a payment dependency can shape which work people can find. You do not have to want every affected game to care about who gets that power.

Keep creation separate from permission to sell.

Creators need original files and finished exports they can access independently of a storefront. A platform library is useful. It should not be your only copy.

ByteWave’s editing tools are free, and local editing does not require an account or a payment. That does not make its publishing service independent of hosting or App Store rules. It means finishing your own video does not require a checkout.

A payment dispute should not take your working copy with it.
