Your Photo Can Carry Your Name, Accounts, and Edit History.

By ByteWave · Published October 7, 2026

AI disclosure: This article includes text generated with an AI language model (LLM). Published by ByteWave, which makes the app discussed here.

The picture is not always everything you are sharing.

Content Credentials can help explain where an image came from. Depending on the tool and settings, they can also carry information about you.

More than an AI label

Adobe’s Photoshop tutorial shows credentials containing a creator’s name, connected social accounts, editing activity, and thumbnails of source images, including the original. The creator chooses which supported information to include.

That is useful for attribution. It also deserves a look before the file leaves your device.

Adobe says linked handles cannot be changed on previously exported content. Disconnecting an account later does not rewrite files you already shared.

Preview before publishing

  1. Open the credentials preview in your editing app. In Photoshop, use the Content Credentials panel or the preview offered during Export As.
  2. Check the identity and history you included. Review names, linked accounts, editing activity, and any source thumbnails.
  3. Choose what to attach. Review the export settings, including whether credentials will also be published to Adobe’s public credentials cloud.

This does not mean every image contains its original or exposes something hidden. Check the actual file and the options you selected.
