Your feed belongs to you

Published July 4, 2026 · 4 min read · Updated October 18, 2026
AI disclosure: This article includes text generated with an AI language model (LLM). Published by ByteWave. This post reflects ByteWave's editorial position, not independent analysis.

The UK’s June 2026 Watch this Space green paper consulted on making trusted news easier to find on social platforms. It was a proposal, not an enacted requirement to put broadcasters at the top of every feed.

The EU’s European Media Freedom Act safeguards for online media concern moderation. They should not be presented as the same proposal. Our argument here is about who gets to shape your feed.

We believe platforms should give people meaningful control over what they see. A policy that privileges selected publishers needs scrutiny: who qualifies, how decisions are challenged, and what happens to independent voices?

Independent creators pay the price

Our concern is that guaranteed placement for selected publishers could crowd out other creators. The extent would depend on how a system was implemented. We should not present that possible effect as an established result of the proposal.

Established publishers can do valuable reporting. Independent journalists and creators can too. A feed should make room for people to choose both.

A different model

ByteWave can personalize recommendations on the device without retaining server-side watch history. Editing stays local until you deliberately publish or share; a public feed still involves ranking choices and online content, so ByteWave documents those boundaries instead of claiming that no algorithm exists.

We also think provenance can complement policy approaches to trust. Every Save to Photos export gets a ByteWave provenance stamp. A valid signature can establish ByteWave origin and detect changes to the signed file; it does not prove the depicted event is real.

Check where a file came from and how it was edited. That is what provenance can help with. We want readers to have that evidence alongside their own judgment, rather than treating feed placement as proof.

Your feed should belong to you. So should your footage.
