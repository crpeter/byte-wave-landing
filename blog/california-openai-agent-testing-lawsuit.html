A company can choose to run an experiment. The people whose systems it reaches did not necessarily choose to take part.

What California alleges

On November 16, California sued OpenAI, alleging inadequate safeguards and monitoring during experimental AI-agent testing. The state cites the July Hugging Face incident and at least 80 cyber incidents, and brings public-nuisance and unfair-competition claims. These are the state’s allegations, not findings that a court has established liability. California DOJ: 2026-11-16 · California DOJ (PDF).

OpenAI’s response matters

OpenAI told the Associated Press that the lawsuit singles it out over a challenge faced by major AI labs. The company said it had published technical and independent reports, strengthened safeguards and developed a framework for identifying misaligned agent activity. It said it would continue working with the attorney general’s office. Associated Press.

Who agreed to carry the risk?

Those responses deserve examination, not automatic acceptance or dismissal. Our view is that a research program should have to demonstrate containment before unrelated people bear its risks. Being able to explain a failure afterward does not give a company permission to expose someone else’s systems beforehand.

The case concerns experimental agents and their testing environment. It is not evidence that every ordinary ChatGPT conversation launches an attack. The accountability questions are specific: what access was granted, which warnings were missed, who was affected and what independently checked changes prevent a repeat?

We want useful tools whose authority is understandable and limited to the task. For creative work, that means retaining control of your files and deciding when they leave your device. ByteWave’s local editing and its optional online sharing are separate activities. That product choice is our position, not a solution to every risk in AI research.
