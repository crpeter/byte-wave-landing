Can Someone Find Your Address From a Photo or Video?

By ByteWave · Published October 1, 2026

AI disclosure: This article includes text generated with an AI language model (LLM). Published by ByteWave, which makes the app discussed here.

Sometimes. Your address does not have to be in the caption.

Check three places.

  • The file: location metadata can record where a photo or video was captured.
  • The picture: a shipping label, street sign, house number, or recognizable view can narrow down a location.
  • The sound: someone may say an address or another identifying detail in the background.

Google’s own location-sharing guidance warns that landmarks can reveal a location even when location sharing is hidden. A sharing setting is not the same as cleaning the file you download and send elsewhere.

Before you share

  1. Watch the entire clip with sound. Check reflections, labels, and background details.
  2. Remove sensitive footage or audio. Inspect the file’s location fields separately.
  3. Export a separate copy. Check that exact copy before sending it.

For supported videos, follow our ByteWave location-metadata tutorial. It covers editable fields in MOV, MP4, and M4V working copies. It does not clean the original photo in your library or promise to remove every kind of metadata.

Removing GPS is only one part.

A visible address stays visible after metadata removal. Information already shared with a platform does not disappear when you edit your local copy.

Keep an original privately if you need its history. Removing metadata can also affect Content Credentials.
