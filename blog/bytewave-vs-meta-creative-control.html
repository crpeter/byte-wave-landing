Local editing works offline. Automatic caption transcription also runs on-device; a speech or language model may need to download first.

Publishing is a separate choice. Online sharing uploads the video you choose to publish. Optional accounts require authentication information, including an email address, and use providers such as Firebase. Our privacy policy explains those boundaries.

Meta’s AI convenience comes with a question: who carries the risk?

Evan Schuman’s October 19 report describes Meta’s upcoming commercial terms, scheduled for October 30, as assigning broad responsibility for AI features and their output to business customers. The report describes contractual responsibility; it does not establish how a court would decide a particular dispute.

Our view: selling automation while putting its mistakes on the customer deserves scrutiny. An AI-generated ad still needs human judgment.

The crawling language changed. The concern remains.

Nieman Lab reported on October 17 that Meta removed the explicit training reference and limited the stated uses of crawled material to advertising and business products. Meta said it respects robots.txt preferences. Legal experts interviewed by Nieman Lab still considered the revised permission too broad.

Meta’s terms preview was login-blocked during our research; the terms discussion above relies on the linked reporting.
