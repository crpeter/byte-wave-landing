You Uploaded a Video. Now Someone Can AI-Remix It.

By ByteWave · Published October 7, 2026

AI disclosure: This article includes text generated with an AI language model (LLM). Published by ByteWave, which makes the app discussed here.

Posting a video should not mean being surprised by an AI version of it.

YouTube’s AI remix tools let people transform eligible Shorts with prompts and images, including inserting themselves into a scene. These remixes carry labels, watermarks, and a link to the source.

YouTube says Shorts are opted into remixing automatically. You can restrict visual remixing.

Turn off video remixing

  1. Open YouTube Studio and select Content.
  2. Open the video you want to change, then expand its settings with Show more.
  3. Find Shorts remixing and select the option that disables video remixing.
  4. Save the change. Review your other uploads too; YouTube also supports bulk changes to this setting.

What does that stop?

YouTube says restricting video remixing removes existing video remixes and prevents new ones through its remix tools. Audio reuse has separate limits; ordinary Shorts creators do not have the same audio opt-out controls as Content Manager partners.

This setting cannot stop someone copying a public video outside those tools. Keep your original recording and exported copy.
