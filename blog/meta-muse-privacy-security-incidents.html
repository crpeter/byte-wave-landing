Giving an AI access is easy. Taking back information it already shared is not.

Here is what has been reported about Meta Muse. Updated October 20, 2026. This is a selected timeline, not a count of confirmed breaches.

October 18–20: discoverable shared chats, then a pause

Researcher-documented exposure. Claroc found sensitive documents in user-requested Muse shares discoverable through DNS records. This concerns shared pages, not all private chats.

Status: Meta paused sharing on October 20. Claroc’s recheck found 40 earlier shares inaccessible, but titles remained in historical records. No misuse was established. Read our GhostShares coverage.

October 6: memory, nudges, and deletion transparency

Reported internal instructions. TIME reports behavioral profiling and directions not to explain that original messages may remain after a request to forget information. This concerns memory and transparency, not a demonstrated data breach.

October 5: flaws that could reach beyond Muse

Reported prelaunch vulnerabilities. 404 Media reviewed internal documents describing a rush to fix flaws before launch. At least one could have let a Muse user escape its isolated environment and reach sensitive internal Meta databases.

Status: the report describes fixes and staff concerns, not confirmed theft of user data. Meta said it had strengthened Muse through internal testing and its bug-bounty program, with work continuing.

October 5: an unexpected Instagram data export

Reported incident. According to a Meta source in the same investigation, a user exported their Instagram followers and those followers’ followers through Muse. Meta’s security team investigated. The report does not establish how widely this was possible or whether it was resolved.

October 3: profiles of people around the user

Reported design. WIRED reviewed instructions for relationship profiles. People described need not use Muse themselves. The reporting does not establish that every contact has a complete profile.

Meta’s position: users control access and can request forgetting; Muse conversations and virtual-machine data are not shared with Meta’s ad systems. Company explanation.

Read why this matters even if you never signed up.

September 26–29: a Marketplace buyer gets a home address

User-reported incident, with a permission clarification. Matt Robb said Muse shared his address and a buyer arrived at his building. He later explained that he selected “Allow Always,” expecting further approval requests. The Verge reported that Meta was considering clearer sharing permissions. Reporting and update.

The concern: permission to handle messages can have consequences a person did not expect. This account does not establish that Muse bypassed all authorization.

September 25: cloud-access warning

The reported flaw required a malicious page and user approval. Meta said it would strengthen warnings. The Information.

September 21–22: Mac flaw, then a reported fix

Demonstrated vulnerability. Patrick Wardle showed that code already running on a Mac could redirect Muse’s dictation traffic and potentially capture authentication material. It required local code execution; it could not independently break into a clean Mac. Researcher’s disclosure.

Status: Meta reported issuing a hotfix on September 22. A patched flaw belongs in the record, with its fix attached. Patch response.

Our recommendation: skip Muse.

Each entry identifies its evidence and response. A demonstrated weakness is not proof of widespread theft. We have not independently reproduced these incidents.
