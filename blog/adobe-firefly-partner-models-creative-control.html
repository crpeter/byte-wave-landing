A video editor should make your next cut easier. Choosing one should not require you to become an expert in every AI company it has invited inside.

What Adobe actually promises

Adobe says its own Firefly models use licensed and public-domain training material. It also says customer content is not used to train its or its partners’ generative models, and that people may choose whether to use generative AI. Those commitments matter. They do not make every partner model an Adobe-trained model with an identical training history. Adobe: Firefly · Adobe: 2026-11-10.

Read the coverage, not just the badge

Adobe’s partner-model guidance makes creators responsible for deciding whether a model suits a project. Its legal documentation also describes separate supplemental coverage for eligible partner-model features under qualifying agreements. Coverage therefore depends on the model, feature and contract. Neither a blanket guarantee nor a blanket claim that everyone is unprotected accurately describes that arrangement. Adobe: 2026-05-08.

Our objection is to the growing homework attached to creative software. A model selector can offer useful choices, but the differences should be visible where people make them. Creators deserve clear explanations of coverage, data handling and whether a feature sends their material to a service.

Keep the editing decision yours

Wanting better color tools, transitions or a faster timeline does not commit you to generating footage. ByteWave’s local editor lets you work on footage on your device without an account; online publishing is separate. We use machine learning for some assistive tools, including captions. The useful question is what a feature does, what access it needs and whether you want it.
