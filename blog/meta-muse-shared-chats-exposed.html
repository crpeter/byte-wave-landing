You Shared a Muse Chat. Strangers Could Find It.

By ByteWave · Published October 20, 2026

AI disclosure: This article includes text generated with an AI language model (LLM). Published by ByteWave, which makes a video feed, editing tools, and provenance tools.

A link meant for one person should not become an invitation to strangers.

Thousands of discoverable links.

Claroc’s October 18 research identified more than 6,800 Muse share addresses through searchable DNS history, records of web-address lookups. Researchers opened a few hundred without logging in.

Some held company revenue, a loan application, banking details, and employees’ vacation schedules. Most pages they checked were harmless.

These were user-requested shares. The finding does not show that every private Muse conversation was exposed.

Meta paused sharing.

On October 20, Meta told Ars Technica it had paused the feature while reviewing the report and considering changes to link discovery.

Claroc rechecked 40 previously accessible shares that day. None opened. Their addresses, including conversation titles, remained in DNS history. The firm reports no evidence of misuse.

Our recommendation: skip Muse.

A pause matters. It does not answer whether anyone else accessed those pages earlier.

Our view: the convenience is not worth giving Meta another route into your personal or work life. Do not connect your accounts or hand Muse your private documents.
