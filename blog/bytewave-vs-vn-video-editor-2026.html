ByteWave vs VN Video Editor: Free Features, Privacy & Translation (2026)

Both ByteWave and VN are capable, watermark-free iPhone video editors. The difference is what “free” means and the kind of editing you want to do. ByteWave offers every editing tool without a paid tier and focuses on local editing and editable Metal effects. VN has a strong multi-track editor, a large template library, and optional paid purchases.

Which app is actually free?

ByteWave has no subscription, no paid editor unlocks and no ByteWave export watermark. Custom shader filters, captions, audio tools, and the new audio-reactive geometry are included without a Pro plan.

VN also advertises watermark-free editing and offers substantial free capabilities. Its U.S. App Store listing, however, includes VN Pro and optional paid credits. That does not mean every feature in VN requires payment; it means the apps use different pricing models. Compare any specific VN feature in the app before assuming it is free.

Keyframes, custom effects and timeline tools

VN is an established option for traditional timeline editing: layers, masks, speed curves, adjustable keyframe curves, beat-synced templates and LUT imports appear in its current App Store description. If you rely on ready-made social video templates, VN may suit you better.

ByteWave's approach centers on creative control. Animate captions and stickers with keyframes; build filters or transitions by editing Metal shader source; use audio-reactive filters and animated 3D geometry; and layer multiple audio tracks. The advanced editing feature guide explains these tools in more detail. These are different strengths, not proof that one editor has the better timeline for every project.

Captions and native translation: an important ByteWave advantage

ByteWave generates automatic captions on the device and uses Apple's native Translation framework for caption translation. Its available translation languages follow the languages supported by Apple's system translation resources and downloaded in iPhone Settings, rather than a short language list maintained inside ByteWave.

For offline translation, download the applicable source and destination languages under Settings → Apps → Translate → Languages, then enable On-Device Mode in Translate settings. Apple's supported languages and translation behavior depend on its system resources and settings; without on-device mode, translation may use Apple's servers. Apple documents the setup.

VN advertises multilingual automatic captions, and its recent release notes describe options for the layout of translated captions. We could not confirm an equivalent system-downloaded-language workflow or a complete current list of supported VN caption translation languages. App interface language counts are not the same thing as caption translation language counts, so we aren't treating them as a valid numeric comparison.

Privacy: read the disclosure, not the slogan

ByteWave performs editing and rendering locally without requiring an account. Its App Store privacy label states “Data Not Collected.” Optional account, sharing, and publishing features involve online services; using them is a separate choice from local editing. See ByteWave's privacy policy.

VN's U.S. App Store privacy label lists identifiers under “Data Used to Track You”, and identifies other data categories used for analytics, marketing, and functionality. That label does not establish that VN uploads your raw video footage during normal editing. Both apps' labels are developer-provided and not independently verified by Apple.

Which should you use?

  • Try ByteWave if you want every editing tool free, no subscription or watermark, local editing, system-language caption translation, and the freedom to write your own GPU effects.
  • Try VN if you want extensive reusable templates, conventional speed ramps and keyframe curves, LUT-based grading, or support for an older iPhone running iOS 16–25.

Neither requires you to choose based on a ranking alone. Test an edit you actually make, including captions, effects, timeline adjustments, and export, then decide which workflow you prefer.
