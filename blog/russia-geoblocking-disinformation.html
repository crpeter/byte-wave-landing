Your feed shouldn’t be a Russian influence operation.

By ByteWave · Published November 9, 2026

AI disclosure: This article was written with AI.

A familiar news logo is easy to copy. Your trust should be harder to borrow.

Reuters reports that an internal EU assessment identified Operation Overload, a covert Russian network, impersonating Western news outlets during France’s unrest. The assessment said the campaign sought to undermine support for Ukraine. It found no evidence Russia started or escalated the protests. Reuters could not independently verify the assessments it reviewed.

Why restrict access?

ByteWave restricts availability in Russia. We stand with Ukraine, and we choose where we distribute our app. Reports of coordinated manipulation reinforce why that choice matters to us.

Geographic restrictions can close a direct route into a service. But the benefit depends on what is blocked: an App Store restriction limits distribution through that storefront; an IP country block rejects connections identified as coming from that country. Those are different controls.

Neither tells you who is behind every account. IP location is an estimate, and an operation can use infrastructure outside Russia. This report does not show that geoblocking would have stopped the campaign.

Check the video, too.

Before sharing a supposed news clip, look for it on the outlet’s own website. In ByteWave, you can also check the original file for C2PA Content Credentials. Supported credentials can provide information about its origin and edits. A signature does not prove the story is true, and missing credentials do not prove it is fake.

We can restrict access and still scrutinize what reaches the feed. A border is not a fact-check.
