Video Editing Apps Compared: ByteWave vs CapCut vs iMovie vs InShot (2026)

Published April 23, 2026 · Updated August 28, 2026 · 5 min read
By Cody Peter, ByteWave founder and sole developer. AI tools assisted drafting and editing. Cody reviewed the product claims; comparisons reflect ByteWave’s commercial interest and are not independent reviews.

Choosing a video editor in 2026 means navigating a landscape of subscriptions, paywalls, and privacy tradeoffs. Here's an honest comparison of four popular options for mobile and Apple ecosystem creators.

Price

ByteWave has no paid tier and adds no ByteWave watermark to exports. iMovie is offered without a separate purchase on supported Apple devices. CapCut and InShot offer free and paid options whose prices, included features, and regional availability can change; check their current store listings before making a purchase decision.

Privacy and data handling

ByteWave's editing and rendering pipeline is local, while optional profiles and deliberate publishing use online services. iMovie is primarily a local editor. CapCut combines local tools with cloud storage, syncing, publishing, and cloud-dependent features. InShot's current privacy disclosures and store privacy labels should be reviewed directly rather than inferred only from whether the app shows ads.

Feature depth

ByteWave offers the deepest feature set for a free app: frame rate conversion, motion blur, temporal noise reduction, resolution upscaling, unlimited audio tracks, voice synthesis, auto captions, hackable shader filters, subject masking, and a community filter library where creators share custom effects. CapCut Pro offers similar breadth but requires a subscription for most advanced features. iMovie is reliable for basic editing but lacks advanced tools. InShot is strong for quick social media edits but limited for complex projects.

Unique differentiators

ByteWave's hackable filter system is genuinely unique - no other mobile editor lets you view and edit shader code, or use AI to create custom effects. CapCut's strength is its integration with TikTok and large template library. iMovie's strength is simplicity and deep Apple ecosystem integration. InShot's strength is speed for quick social cuts.

Platform support

ByteWave runs on iPhone, iPad, and Mac (Apple Silicon required). CapCut is available on iOS, Android, Windows, Mac, and Web. iMovie runs on iPhone, iPad, and Mac. InShot is available on iOS and Android.

The bottom line

If you use supported Apple hardware and value local editing, editable Metal filters, and no paid tier, ByteWave is designed for that combination. Cross-platform support, collaboration, templates, and familiarity may make another editor a better fit. Recheck current product pages before deciding because competitor pricing and features change.
