Choosing a video editor in 2026 means navigating a landscape of subscriptions, paywalls, and privacy tradeoffs. Here's an honest comparison of four popular options for mobile and Apple ecosystem creators.

Price

ByteWave has no paid tier and adds no ByteWave watermark to exports. iMovie is offered without a separate purchase on supported Apple devices. CapCut and InShot offer free and paid options whose prices, included features, and regional availability can change; check their current store listings before making a purchase decision.

Privacy and data handling

ByteWave's editing and rendering pipeline is local, while optional profiles and deliberate publishing use online services. iMovie is primarily a local editor. CapCut combines local tools with cloud storage, syncing, publishing, and cloud-dependent features. InShot's current privacy disclosures and store privacy labels should be reviewed directly rather than inferred only from whether the app shows ads.

Feature depth

ByteWave’s free tools include frame rate conversion, motion blur, temporal noise reduction, resolution upscaling, unlimited audio tracks, voice synthesis, auto captions, hackable shader filters, subject masking, and a community filter library where creators share custom effects. CapCut lists desktop image noise reduction in Pro; full auto-caption access also requires Pro. See the paid-feature breakdown. iMovie is reliable for basic editing but lacks advanced tools. InShot is strong for quick social media edits but limited for complex projects.

Unique differentiators

ByteWave lets you view and edit Metal filter code, including code you develop with an AI assistant. CapCut's strength is its integration with TikTok and large template library. iMovie's strength is simplicity and deep Apple ecosystem integration. InShot's strength is speed for quick social cuts.

What about VN Video Editor?

VN Video Editor is another capable free iPhone option, with multi-track editing, keyframe curves, effects, and reusable templates. Its U.S. App Store listing also offers optional VN Pro and credit purchases. ByteWave instead makes all editing tools free, including editable Metal effects, and uses Apple's downloaded system languages for native caption translation. For a detailed feature and privacy comparison, read ByteWave vs VN Video Editor (2026).

Platform support

ByteWave runs on any iPhone supporting iOS 26, iPads with iPadOS 26, and Macs with macOS 26 and M1 or later. CapCut is available on iOS, Android, Windows, Mac, and Web. iMovie runs on iPhone, iPad, and Mac. InShot is available on iOS and Android.

The bottom line

If you use supported Apple hardware and value local editing, editable Metal filters, and no paid tier, ByteWave is designed for that combination. Cross-platform support, collaboration, templates, and familiarity may make another editor a better fit. Recheck current product pages before deciding because competitor pricing and features change.
