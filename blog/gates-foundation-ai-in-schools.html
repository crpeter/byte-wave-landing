Hasn’t Gates done enough to harm children?

By ByteWave · Published November 3, 2026

AI disclosure: This article includes text generated with an AI language model (LLM).

In California charter schools that took part in a Gates-funded teacher evaluation program, students in grades 3–8 did worse in math than their peers at comparable schools. High school English scores improved. Across the initiative, though, the promised gains in achievement and graduation didn’t materialize. The foundation put in $212 million. Districts put up money, too. The total bill was $575 million.

Now the Gates Foundation has earmarked about $400 million for AI in education. It says independent researchers will evaluate the tools. But a third-grade teacher who wants a math tutor instead of a chatbot has already lost the first decision: the money is for AI.

Gates’s record at Microsoft gives us reason to care about who gets a choice. A federal appeals court upheld the finding that Microsoft illegally maintained its PC operating-system monopoly. In his videotaped antitrust deposition, Gates repeatedly pressed the government’s lawyer to define support. To us, he sounded evasive.

The foundation has picked some unusual bets for children before. In 2012, it committed $100,000 to All Things Bugs to study insect powder as a food for children facing malnutrition. The grant tells us what the foundation wanted to test. It doesn’t tell us what families would have bought with the same money.

That comparison is still possible in schools. Give teachers the same budget. If they want another adult at the math table, pay for one. Then see which kids catch up.
