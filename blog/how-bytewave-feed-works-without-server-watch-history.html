How ByteWave Personalizes a Video Feed Without Server-Side Watch History

By ByteWave · Published 2026-08-28 · Updated 2026-08-28

ByteWave reviewed product and policy claims against the linked primary sources; AI tools assisted drafting and editing.

The four data boundaries

1. EditRaw footage remains in the local editing pipeline and is not uploaded to ByteWave.
2. AuthenticateAn optional profile uses Firebase Authentication; authentication information is distinct from footage.
3. PublishA video is uploaded only when the user deliberately publishes or shares through an online feature.
4. BrowseRecommendation signals and liked-video history remain local; ByteWave receives no server-side watch history.

Without a profile

People can edit and render locally and browse the public feed anonymously. ByteWave does not maintain server-side watch history for anonymous or signed-in browsing.

What a profile changes

Profiles support optional social participation. Firebase Authentication handles account authentication. Published content and profile information necessarily use online services; that does not make local editing an upload workflow.

Personalization and likes

Personalized recommendations are optional and computed on the device. The “liked videos” history described in the privacy policy is also stored locally. A profile does not turn those local behavioral records into server watch history.

Apple-provided processing

Some Apple-provided capabilities, including speech recognition in some configurations, may send information to Apple depending on the device, language, feature, availability, and system settings. That processing boundary is different from ByteWave receiving raw editing footage.

What this architecture does—and does not—show

It supports a narrow claim: ByteWave can offer local editing and optional feed personalization without retaining server-side watch history or using behavioral data for targeted advertising. Reduced collection alone does not prove wellbeing, child safety, or freedom from compulsive-use risk.
