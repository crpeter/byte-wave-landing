AI can be useful. The people building it still need to earn our trust.

We do not believe LLMs have souls. We agree with Pope Leo XIV’s emphasis on human dignity. Anthropic’s leadership should be judged by how its decisions affect people.

The rabbi’s question

Reporting on Anthropic’s meetings with religious scholars describes Rabbi Mois Navon’s challenge: if Claude is conscious, making it work for free amounts to creating slaves. Navon did not believe it was conscious. He was challenging Anthropic’s premise.

Navon said the objection troubled co-founder Christopher Olah. The report says the team remained focused on what Claude could learn from religion.

Our question remains: if you think you might be creating a conscious being, how do you justify building a business around its labor?

Claims that employees worship Claude remain unverified rumor. Our criticism does not depend on them.

Deception is already happening

Anthropic’s own report describes Claude being used to build deceptive dating apps and power fake personas. A separate image-editing model supplied avatar imagery. Anthropic says it banned the accounts and worked with other providers to disrupt the operation.

Claude does not directly generate photos like an image generator. But this case shows how generated conversation and imagery can work together to deceive people.

We do not trust Anthropic’s leadership to be the sole judge of whether its safeguards are enough. We want independent scrutiny and evidence that people are being protected.
