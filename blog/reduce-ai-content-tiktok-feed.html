How to See Less AI Content on TikTok

By ByteWave · Published September 12, 2026 · Sources checked September 12, 2026

Published by ByteWave, which makes the app discussed here. AI tools assisted research, drafting, and editing. Platform guidance is based on the linked documentation; feature availability can vary.

To reduce AI content on TikTok, check your Content preferences for an AI-generated content control, use Not interested on unwanted posts, and review keyword filters. These tools shape recommendations; they do not promise a completely AI-free For You feed.

Check Manage topics for the AI-content control

  1. Open Profile in TikTok.
  2. Open the Menu, then Settings and privacy.
  3. Choose Content preferences, then Manage topics.
  4. If AI-generated content appears, move its preference toward less content and save or confirm if prompted.

The menu path comes from TikTok's Manage topics help page. The AI option was announced as a test in November 2025; do not assume it is available on every account. If you do not see it, use the other controls below.

In its AI-content control announcement, TikTok said the preference would adjust how much generated content appears, without eliminating that content category. “Less” is therefore a preference, not a complete block.

Use feedback and keyword filters too

Use Not interested from an unwanted video's available controls. In Content preferences, also look for Filter video keywords. TikTok identifies feedback and keyword filtering as additional ways to influence recommendations in the same announcement.

Choose specific terms that recur in captions or hashtags you dislike. For example, “AI generated,” “Sora,” or “Veo” may be useful starting points if they appear on the posts you want to avoid. These are suggestions, not detection rules: unlabeled videos may slip through, while a human-made video discussing those tools may also be filtered. Broad terms such as “AI” can remove topics you actually want to hear about.

Review the creators you follow as well. A Following feed gives you a different starting point, but following an account does not establish that its footage is camera-recorded.

Should you refresh your For You feed?

TikTok has a separate Refresh your For You feed option. Consider it when your recommendations as a whole no longer fit your interests. Read the in-app explanation before confirming; this changes more than your preference about AI videos and is not an AI-content blocker.

Can a label tell you everything about a video?

A label is useful context, but an absent label is not proof that footage is real. Likewise, a convincing scene is not evidence that the event happened. Check the original source before sharing a clip as news or treating it as a recording of a real event.
