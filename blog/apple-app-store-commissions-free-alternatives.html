Apple Can’t Take a Cut of a Subscription You Don’t Need.

By ByteWave · Published October 7, 2026

AI disclosure: This article includes text generated with an AI language model (LLM). Published by ByteWave, which makes the app discussed here.

When a free tool replaces a paid subscription, the customer keeps their money. Apple loses whatever commission it would have collected.

That is what makes developers building free alternatives interesting. Some of us would rather make the feature free than build another subscription business around it.

How much money are we talking about?

Appfigures estimated that Apple collected about $28 billion in App Store developer fees in 2024. That is an outside estimate of fees, not Apple’s entire Services revenue.

Here is what reductions against that historical baseline would mean:

Illustrative annual revenue losses, not forecasts
Commission revenue displacedApple revenue lost
5%$1.4 billion
10%$2.8 billion
25%$7 billion

Free downloads alone do not create those losses. People must replace purchases they would otherwise have made, without shifting that spending to another commission-paying app.

Building it is getting interesting

AI may lower the effort needed to build alternatives. Reverse engineering offers another route to understanding existing software. One AI-assisted Halo project describes its work as a best-effort reconstruction, not recovery of the original source line for line.

Working code still needs maintenance. Rebuilding an app also does not recreate its servers, content library, or community. But useful local tools do not all need that infrastructure.

Apple could still benefit from free apps making its devices more useful, while new paid apps create new commissions. The figures above do not measure those offsets.

Make the feature free

You should not need a recurring bill just to put captions on your own footage.
