TikTok’s Safety Staff Raised the Alarm. What Did Leadership Do?

By ByteWave · Published October 20, 2026

AI disclosure: This article includes text generated with an AI language model (LLM). Published by ByteWave, which makes a video feed, editing tools, and provenance tools.

When safety staff raise the alarm, families deserve to know what leadership did next.

The warnings came from inside.

NPR reported on October 20 that previously unreported employee depositions described repeated child-safety warnings inside TikTok. The testimony was filed in March 2026. Some concerns dated to 2020.

Former safety and investigations staffer Reagen Maher described spending months escalating concerns about sexual exploitation involving minors on TikTok Live, including its virtual-gift system.

Former safety head Eric Han described harmful recommendation loops involving suicide and body-image content. He testified that TikTok did not warn parents about the potential dangers. The Verge also covered the testimony.

What TikTok said.

NPR said TikTok declined to provide a statement after extended communications and directed reporters to existing company policies.

These are employees’ accounts in litigation, not a jury’s findings. NPR reported that the depositions had not yet been presented as evidence at trial.

Show what changed.

Our view: a published safety policy cannot tell families whether an internal warning led to action.

Who received the warnings? What changed afterward? How did TikTok check whether those changes worked?

Those questions deserve specific answers. The same standard applies to every platform, including ByteWave. Privacy features alone do not establish that a service is safe for children.
