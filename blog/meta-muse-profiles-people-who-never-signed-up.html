You Didn’t Sign Up. Your Friend Did.

By ByteWave · Published October 7, 2026

AI disclosure: This article includes text generated with an AI language model (LLM). Published by ByteWave, which makes video editing and provenance tools.

Someone else’s permission can expose your private life.

You can choose not to use Muse. That does not decide what a friend tells it about you.

Your life, in someone else’s AI memory.

WIRED’s October 3 report describes internal instructions for profiles of people in a user’s life, including relationship history. This is evidence of the design, not proof that every contact already has a complete dossier.

TIME’s October 6 investigation also describes behavioral nudges and instructions discouraging Muse from explaining that forgetting information may leave the original message visible.

Whose permission counts?

Meta says users choose what Muse can access and can ask it to forget information. It says conversations and data in a user’s virtual machine are not shared with its advertising systems.

Those assurances still leave a question: what choice did the person being described get?

Your inbox contains other people’s confidences. A conversation about a family problem is not just data about you.

Don’t connect it.

Our recommendation is to not use Muse or give it access to private communications. Other people’s lives are in there too.

Already connected? Revoke its access and review stored data separately. Disconnecting cannot undo past disclosures or control what someone else supplies.
