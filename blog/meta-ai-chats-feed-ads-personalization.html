You Asked a Chatbot. Why Did Your Feed Change?

By ByteWave · Published October 6, 2026

AI disclosure: This article includes text generated with an AI language model (LLM). Published by ByteWave, which makes the app discussed here.

An answer is not necessarily the only thing you get from a chatbot conversation. You can also give an advertising system another clue about yourself.

In October 2025, Meta announced that interactions with its AI features would help personalize content and ads, with changes taking effect December 16, 2025 in most regions.

Its own example: talk to Meta AI about hiking, and you might see hiking groups, trail posts, or hiking-boot ads. Voice and text interactions can both provide signals.

A question can become an interest

Asking about something does not necessarily mean you want more of it in your feed. You might be helping a friend, researching a problem, or simply curious.

Our concern is that the familiar act of asking for help doubles as information for a recommendation system. People deserve to understand that before starting the conversation.

What Meta says it limits

Meta says it excludes certain sensitive conversation topics from ad targeting, including health, religion, and political views. It also describes limits on cross-account use tied to which accounts you connect in Accounts Center.

Those restrictions matter. They do not make an AI conversation separate from Meta’s advertising business.

If you do not want your questions feeding that system, skip Meta AI. Meta points to feed and ad preferences for adjusting recommendations; those controls are not a promise that the conversation stays unused.
