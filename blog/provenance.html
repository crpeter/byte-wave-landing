Provenance, not prominence

How ByteWave stamps your exports

Every time you save to Photos, ByteWave adds its own provenance stamp. It carries signed ByteWave history and source assessments. It is metadata, not a visible watermark.

To inspect a saved copy, import that file into ByteWave, select its clip, and tap the provenance badge. If badges are hidden, use Show Badges. Read the details, not just the badge color. See the checking steps and results.

ByteWave’s stamp is separate from the C2PA Content Credentials it can read in imported files. A valid signature helps establish the signed record’s origin and detect changes to what was signed. It does not prove that the scene happened.

Our view: provenance, not prominence

Making a publisher easier to find does not make every clip it publishes true.

The UK’s June 2026 Watch this Space green paper consulted on making public-service media and other trusted news more prominent online. It did not itself enact a rule forcing broadcasters to the top of every feed. The EU’s Article 18 media safeguards address moderation of qualifying media providers, a different mechanism.

Our concern with publisher prominence is simple: ranking an institution higher is not evidence about an individual file. Readers still need ways to check the source and editing history of what they see.

This treats discoverability as a proxy for trustworthiness. But those are different problems. Making something easy to find doesn't make it true. Making something hard to find doesn't make it false.

The actual problem

You cannot reliably determine a video's history just by looking at it. A synthetic clip and a camera recording may appear similar in a feed. Provenance can add a machine-verifiable signal about origin and edits, but it cannot establish that the depicted event happened.

Publisher identity can be useful context. It should not replace checking a particular claim or dismissing footage solely because an independent creator published it.

What provenance solves

Content provenance takes a different approach. Instead of asking only "who published this?" it asks what can be verified about the file's origin and modification history.

ByteWave applies its own signed stamp when saving to Photos. The practical checking steps are above. ByteWave’s own stamp and imported C2PA credentials are separate.

The C2PA standard defines a way to attach verifiable content history. ByteWave reads supported C2PA credentials and also creates its own export stamp. These are distinct capabilities.

Two models of trust

Our concern is that giving selected publishers privileged placement could reduce opportunities for independent voices. That is a policy argument, not a measured effect of the UK proposal.

A provenance system supplies evidence that readers and platforms can inspect. It still depends on trusted signing keys, secure implementations, clear disclosure, and careful interpretation; cryptography alone cannot certify the truth of a scene.

A useful question alongside publisher prominence is: how do we give every creator tools to document a file's origin and editing history?

That question has a technical answer. We're building it.
