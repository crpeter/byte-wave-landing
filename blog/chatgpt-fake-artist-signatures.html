A Real Artist’s Name on a Picture They Never Made.

By ByteWave · Published October 7, 2026

AI disclosure: This article includes text generated with an AI language model (LLM). Published by ByteWave, which makes video editing and provenance tools.

Your name should belong to you.

On October 5, 2026, Nieman Lab reported that ChatGPT-generated cartoons carried the signatures of more than 15 real New Yorker cartoonists. Some appeared after generic requests for the magazine’s style.

Brendan Loper received messages asking whether one generated cartoon was his. It wasn’t.

A name carries someone’s reputation.

An invented picture with a real signature can make it look like someone made a joke, endorsed an idea, or produced work they never touched.

OpenAI’s response described reports like these as bugs and unintended behavior. Nieman Lab said some generated cartoons still carried real signatures at publication.

Creators should not have to keep proving what they didn’t make.

Check where it came from.

Find the work on the artist’s own site or an original publisher’s page. A screenshot with a familiar name is not enough.

A drawn signature is part of the picture. C2PA Content Credentials are signed records about a file’s origin and edits. They can add evidence, but do not automatically prove an artist’s identity or that a depicted event happened.
