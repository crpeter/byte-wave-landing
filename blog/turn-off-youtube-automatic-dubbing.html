Translation can help your video reach people. You should still get to approve what it says.

YouTube’s automatic dubbing documentation says the feature is enabled by default for eligible creators. It also acknowledges mistakes with speech recognition, names, idioms, and translation.

A fluent voice can make an error sound confident. Your audience may hear it as your words.

Turn off automatic dubbing

  1. On a computer, open YouTube Studio.
  2. Go to Settings → Channel → Advanced settings.
  3. Clear the checkbox for Allow automatic dubbing, then save your settings.

Want to review translations instead?

Keep dubbing enabled and select Manually review dubs before publishing in the same settings. Have someone who understands the language check the meaning before approval.

Already have a dub you do not want?

In Studio, select the video under Content, then open Languages. Find the language’s Audio menu and choose Unpublish or Delete. Unpublishing allows you to restore it later; deletion does not.

YouTube does not let you edit automatic dubs. Review existing tracks separately from changing your channel settings.

Your original voice still works.

You can keep your recorded audio and add captions you have checked yourself. ByteWave’s caption tutorial shows the controls, and text effects give the words a look of their own.

While reviewing your channel, check whether your Shorts allow AI video remixing too.
