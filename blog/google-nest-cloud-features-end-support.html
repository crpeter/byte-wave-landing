Buying the hardware does not guarantee that its connected features will stay.

Early Nest owners found the limit.

Google ended support on October 25, 2025 for first-generation Nest Learning Thermostats, second-generation models, and the second-generation European model.

Those thermostats lost their connection to the Nest and Google Home apps. Remote control, notifications, and other connected features stopped. Software and security updates ended too.

The heating controls did not all stop working. Owners can still change temperature, settings, and schedules on the thermostat itself. This affected specific older models, not every Nest thermostat.

The useful question: what survives?

Our view: the offline fallback should be part of the buying decision from day one.

A device can still function while losing features that helped sell it. A creative app can leave you with files while removing the convenient way to open, edit, or export them. “You own it” tells you less than “here is what works when our service ends.”

Before committing, ask three plain questions:

  • Which features work with the internet off?
  • Can I keep my files somewhere else?
  • What exactly happens when support ends?

Apply that test to your editor.

ByteWave lets you edit local footage and save to Photos without signing in or connecting. Keep your original clips as well as finished exports. An exported video preserves the result, not an editable copy of every project decision.

Offline operation is useful today. It is not a promise that any app will run forever on future hardware or operating systems.

Ask what still works after the service stops answering. Start with a simple offline editing test.
