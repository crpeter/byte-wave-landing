You Made It. Should AI Companies Get to Train on It?

By ByteWave · Published September 22, 2026

AI disclosure: This article includes text generated with an AI language model (LLM). Published by ByteWave, which makes the app discussed here.

You filmed it. You edited it. You decided to share it. Did you agree to help train someone else’s AI?

On September 22, 2026, Reuters reported that OpenAI and Anthropic were urging Australia to relax copyright restrictions on AI training. Their submissions sought a framework allowing some use of copyrighted material. This is a policy proposal, not a newly enacted permission. Read the Reuters report.

Creators deserve a real choice.

Our view: making something public should not mean surrendering a meaningful say in how it is used. Companies building products from creative work should seek permission and offer fair terms.

Start with where your footage goes.

ByteWave lets you edit on your device without uploading your footage for editing. You can work locally and choose what to publish. Our account-free editing guide explains that separation.

Once you publish elsewhere, that platform’s terms and other people’s actions matter. Local editing cannot prevent someone copying a public post. Removing metadata cannot prevent AI training either.

Useful tools should help you make your work—and respect your choices about it.
