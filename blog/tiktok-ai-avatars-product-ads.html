That Person Selling You Something Might Be Generated.

By ByteWave · Published October 7, 2026

AI disclosure: This article includes text generated with an AI language model (LLM). Published by ByteWave, which makes the app discussed here.

A person holds up a product. They smile. They tell you how good it is. That scene might never have happened.

TikTok’s Symphony tools include digital presenters based on licensed actors and avatars that showcase products. Advertisers can generate footage from text or existing images. TikTok says Creative Studio outputs carry an AI-generated label.

A performance is not proof.

On October 5, 2026, TikTok announced another way to bring generated ads and creator footage into the same campaign approval process.

The concern is simple: a generated demonstration cannot establish how a product performs in real life. A convincing face and a confident script do not establish personal experience.

An AI label helps explain how the video was made. It does not verify the sales pitch.

Before you believe the demonstration

  • Check who is speaking. Is this a named reviewer, a paid presenter, or a generated character?
  • Look for actual use. Can you find independent footage showing the claimed result?
  • Inspect available evidence. Content Credentials may identify AI generation. Missing credentials prove neither truth nor fakery.
