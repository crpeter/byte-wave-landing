Meta Muse Privacy: Why We Recommend Not Using It

By ByteWave · Published September 19, 2026 · Updated September 22, 2026

AI disclosure: This article includes text generated with an AI language model (LLM). Published by ByteWave, which makes the app discussed here. This article states ByteWave's editorial position, grounded in the linked Meta documentation. Product details may change.

Our recommendation is to not use Meta Muse and not connect your accounts to it. If you do not want Facebook's parent company to gain access to more of your personal data, do not give it another route into your inbox, calendar, files, or daily life.

Meta Muse is Meta's personal AI agent. This article explains why we recommend opting out of the product altogether. You do not need to find the right combination of settings to justify saying no.

September 22 update: Amazon blocks Muse

Business Insider reports that Amazon blocked Muse and raised concerns about unauthorized account access and customer credentials. Those are Amazon’s allegations, not independently established findings of a breach.

Our recommendation remains the same: do not connect your personal accounts to Muse. You can decline that access without waiting for companies to settle their dispute.

Why we recommend staying away from Muse

Meta's technical documentation says the launch version can be accessed by Meta when necessary to support, secure, or operate the service. It describes sanitized interaction data being used for training unless users opt out, and limited data leaving the VM for inference and telemetry. Source: Meta's security and data explanation.

That is enough for our recommendation. We do not think the convenience is worth extending this relationship to more of your personal information. Choosing not to participate is a valid privacy decision, even when a company offers permission controls and assurances about how it handles data.

Our standard here is straightforward: we do not want to give Meta access to this material. A settings checklist cannot satisfy that goal while encouraging you to hand the material over. Read-only access is still access to what you allow the service to read.

Why the advertised safeguards do not change our view

Meta describes account-permission controls, approvals for sensitive actions, and separation of credentials from the agent. It also says Muse conversations and VM data are not shared with Meta's ad systems. Those are specific protections; they are not a promise of no data access or processing. Source: Meta's launch announcement.

Meta also acknowledges that Muse can make mistakes and that prompt injection remains an open problem. Its isolation and Sentinel systems are designed to limit the impact. Source: Meta's security design.

We are not comfortable recommending that readers connect sensitive accounts on the strength of those safeguards. You can avoid granting Muse that access in the first place. Our advice is to skip the service, rather than start small and gradually entrust it with more.

A future privacy promise is not a reason to hand over data now

Meta's September 8 launch announcement describes Muse Confidential VM as a future release intended to prevent Meta from accessing the VM's contents. That is distinct from the launch version of Secure VM. Source: Meta's Confidential VM announcement.

We would evaluate any delivered version on its actual behavior. An announced future protection does not change our current recommendation to avoid Muse.

If you already connected accounts

Stop providing Muse with additional personal information. Review and revoke its access in Muse and in the connected services' own account settings. Review available deletion controls and retention information for material you have already supplied.

Treat revoking future access and deleting previously stored data as separate tasks. Do not assume that disconnecting a service erases every copy or reverses past processing. These are exit steps, not instructions for continuing to use Muse safely.

Avoiding Muse prevents you from voluntarily providing data through that product. It does not erase information Meta already holds or stop collection through other Meta products you continue to use.

You can choose tools without handing over your daily life

For us, useful software should earn its place by doing the job you asked for with as little access as possible. You are allowed to choose a smaller, more specific tool—or do a task yourself—without connecting your personal accounts to an AI agent.
