If you've been following the CapCut situation, you know it's been a rollercoaster. CapCut is owned by ByteDance, the same Chinese company behind TikTok. That connection has put it directly in the crosshairs of U.S. legislation targeting foreign-adversary-controlled applications.

What actually happened

CapCut was temporarily unavailable in the United States in January 2025 alongside other ByteDance services and later returned. Availability and enforcement status can change, so this page does not treat an old store check as a permanent answer.

The governing U.S. statute covers certain foreign-adversary-controlled applications, and legal or ownership developments can affect services differently. For a current answer, check the U.S. App Store and authoritative government or court notices on the day you need the app.

Can you still download CapCut right now?

Use CapCut's official store listing as the current availability check. Regardless of today's status, creators can reduce workflow risk by keeping local copies of source media and exported projects and by knowing a second editor.

The bigger problem beyond the ban

Availability is only one decision factor. CapCut's free and paid features, cloud services, and terms can change; review its current store listing, privacy policy, and terms rather than relying on a fixed price or a blanket upload claim.
