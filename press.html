100 words

ByteWave is a free video editor and social feed for iPhone, iPad, and Apple Silicon Macs, built by a single developer as a privacy-first alternative to the dominant short-form editing apps. Video processing runs on the device rather than in the cloud, and its App Store privacy label reads "Data Not Collected." Story angles

Three framings, depending on what you cover.

The privacy alternative

The market-leading short-form video editor is owned by ByteDance, and its handling of biometric and content-licensing data has drawn ongoing regulatory scrutiny. ByteWave is the same category of tool built on the opposite premise: processing stays on the device, and the App Store privacy label reads "Data Not Collected." No feature in the app requires uploading a face or a voice to a server.

What a hard minimum requirement buys you

ByteWave requires iOS 26 and supports nothing older. That is an unusual choice for a consumer app chasing installs, and it is deliberate: it lets the app use current Apple frameworks directly instead of maintaining fallbacks. Captions, translation, Metal-based effects, and export all run locally as a result, and the same build runs on Apple Silicon Macs. The tradeoff is a smaller addressable device base in exchange for capability no cross-platform competitor can match.

Free, and not the freemium kind

Every major competitor in this category runs the same play: free until the moment it matters, then a watermark on the export, or a resolution cap, or a paywalled caption style. ByteWave has no Pro tier waiting behind the export button, no watermark, and no upsell. The app a first-time user opens is the whole app. Built and run by one person.
