How to Remove Location Metadata in ByteWave

By ByteWave · Published September 22, 2026

AI disclosure: This article includes text generated with an AI language model (LLM). Published by ByteWave, which makes the app discussed here.

A video can carry coordinates you never meant to share. Check the file before you send it.

On September 21, 2026, Ireland’s Data Protection Commission announced a €403 million Google fine over location-data processing. That case concerns Google’s services, not photo metadata. It is a reminder of why location privacy matters. Read the regulator’s announcement.

Remove the fields you choose.

These controls work on MOV, MP4, and M4V working files in ByteWave. Your original stays unchanged.

  1. Select your clip on the editor timeline and open More info.
  2. Check Recorded location. If present, open entries such as Coordinates to see their stored values. Review the other metadata sections too.
  3. Choose Remove this field for an editable entry. To remove all editable fields together, use Remove editable metadata on the main information screen.
  4. Read the confirmation and tap Remove. ByteWave creates a new working copy and refreshes the list. Check that the selected fields are gone.
  5. Export, then inspect the saved video. Import that export as a new clip and open More info again. Share the checked export rather than your original.

Know what stays.

Read-only fields, track metadata, and timed metadata are preserved. Exports may add ByteWave provenance stamps. This is not a promise to erase every kind of metadata.

Imported photos may be converted to video. Their original photo metadata may not appear in this viewer. This workflow does not clean the original photo in your library.

Rewriting a file can remove or invalidate embedded Content Credentials. Keep the original if you need its provenance. The Recorded at import assessment describes that original, not a fresh check of the edited copy.

Metadata removal cannot hide street signs, spoken addresses, or locations visible in the footage. For another privacy boundary worth checking, read Does an Emoji Really Protect Your Child’s Face on Facebook?
