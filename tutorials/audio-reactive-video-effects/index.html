ByteWave's audio-reactive effects use analyzed sound to animate visual effects and geometry. You can choose these effects in the filter browser without writing shader code. A quiet passage and a strong musical section can produce different results with the same preset.

Prepare a short edit with audio

Open a video project and use Audio to add or arrange your audio. Start with a short section containing both a quieter passage and a clear change in energy. Let audio analysis finish before judging the effect, and check that the audio covers the part of the timeline you are previewing.

Keep your first edit simple enough to review: one video clip, one audio track and one effect. This makes it easier to hear and see which moments are driving the result.

Choose an effect in Filter → Audio

  1. Select the video clip you want to change.
  2. Open Filter and select the Audio category in the filter browser.
  3. Browse the animated previews. Tap a preset's preview tile to apply it to the selected clip.
  4. Look for the selected tile's checkmark, close the filter browser and play the edit.

The Audio category inside Filters contains visual effects. The editor's separate Audio tool is where you work with the soundtrack. Applying a visual preset does not add a music track.

Try presets with different behavior

  • Prism Ribbons: luminous ribbons with slow movement and a gentle audio response.
  • Pulse Globe: a rotating wireframe sphere that pulses with audio and remains visible during silence.
  • Spectral Halo: layered contours shaped by audio history, appearing while analyzed audio is active.
  • Spectral Silk: woven filaments and translucent fields shaped by audio history.

These are preset names from the English interface; names are localized in the app. Compare the same section of your edit with more than one preset. Choose the effect that leaves your subject and captions readable.

Check the response through the whole clip

Play a quiet section, a louder section and the transition between them. Audio-driven movement is not a promise that every shape will hit every beat: presets can respond to overall energy or different frequency ranges, and some also have continuous animation.

If the geometry keeps moving in silence, that can be intentional. If an audio-dependent effect disappears or barely responds, check that analysis has completed and that analyzed audio is active at that point in the edit. Try Pulse Globe as a comparison because its base shape remains visible in silence.

Change or remove an effect

Return to Filter → Audio and tap another preview to change the selected clip's effect. To clear it, open the filter browser's options menu and choose Remove Filter.

For custom work, the preset's edit control opens the shader editor. You do not need it for this tutorial. The Metal shader guide explains the code behind customizable effects.

Export a short version and review it

Export your short edit, then open the saved video with sound. Check that the visual response fits the soundtrack, your subject remains visible and text stays readable. Repeat that review after changing the track's position or the edit's timing.
