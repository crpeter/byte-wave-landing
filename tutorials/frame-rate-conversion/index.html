What frame rate conversion changes

Slowing a clip stretches the time between its captured frames. A 30 fps recording played at one-tenth speed supplies only about three captured frames per second of playback. Repeating those frames in a higher-frame-rate export does not add new positions of the moving subject.

Frame interpolation estimates additional images between captured frames. Those intermediate images can make slowed movement appear more continuous. Apple describes frame rate conversion as supporting clip retiming and slow motion.

In this example, the source is approximately 30 fps and three seconds long. Both sides of the comparison are presented in the same 60 fps video. That shared output frame rate makes it easier to compare the two methods; it does not mean every displayed frame is a distinct captured or generated image.

Make a fair comparison with your own clip

  1. Keep an untouched original. Start with a short clip so you can inspect the entire result.
  2. Choose the same speed for both versions. For this example, use 0.1× for the ordinary slowdown and for ByteWave’s frame rate conversion.
  3. Process from the original. Use the original clip as the input to frame rate conversion, rather than slowing an already-slowed export again.
  4. Watch both results at their intended speed. Compare smoothness, then pause to check edges and fine detail before choosing which version to keep.

What to check around fast movement

Hummingbird wings are a demanding subject because their position can change substantially between captured frames. Watch for doubled edges, smearing, or distortion around the bird and feeder. The goal is a result that works in motion while keeping the subject convincing.

Interpolated frames are estimates, so use this as a creative editing comparison. If a section looks distorted, try a less aggressive slowdown and compare again.

Frame rate conversion and motion blur

Frame rate conversion adds intermediate images for retiming. Motion blur changes the appearance of movement within images. See the motion-blur tutorial for a separate example and recorded walkthrough.
