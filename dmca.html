Copyright & DMCA

Last updated: October 19, 2026

ByteWave respects copyright and the rights of creators. This policy explains how copyright owners and their authorized representatives can report material on ByteWave, and how users can respond if their material is removed by mistake.

Report copyright infringement

If you own a copyright or are authorized to act for its owner, send our agent a written notice containing all of the following. For email, use the subject “ByteWave DMCA notice.”

  1. Your physical or electronic signature as the copyright owner or a person authorized to act on the owner's behalf.
  2. Identification of the copyrighted work you believe has been infringed. If one notice covers multiple works at a single online site, you may provide a representative list.
  3. Identification of the material you want removed or disabled, and enough information for us to locate it. Include the specific URL when available. For in-app material, include the post or filter identifier, creator username, and any other details needed to find it.
  4. Information reasonably sufficient for us to contact you, such as your mailing address, telephone number, and email address, if available.
  5. A statement that you have a good faith belief that use of the material in the manner complained of is not authorized by the copyright owner, its agent, or the law.
  6. A statement that the information in the notice is accurate and, under penalty of perjury, that you are authorized to act on behalf of the owner of an exclusive right that is allegedly infringed.

Consider whether the use is authorized by a license or by law, including fair use, before submitting a notice. Copyright complaints should identify copyright infringement, rather than disagreement with a viewpoint.

We respond expeditiously to notices that meet the DMCA's requirements by removing or disabling access to the identified material. We take reasonable steps to promptly notify the affected user. If a notice is incomplete, we may contact the sender for the missing information.

If your material was removed by mistake

If you believe material you posted was removed or disabled because of a mistake or misidentification, you may send our agent a written counter-notice. For email, use the subject “ByteWave DMCA counter-notice.” Include:

  1. Your physical or electronic signature.
  2. Identification of the material that was removed or disabled and the location where it appeared before removal or disabling.
  3. A statement under penalty of perjury that you have a good faith belief that the material was removed or disabled as a result of mistake or misidentification.
  4. Your name, mailing address, and telephone number.
  5. A statement that you consent to the jurisdiction of the Federal District Court for the judicial district where your address is located. If your address is outside the United States, state that you consent to the jurisdiction of any judicial district in which ByteWave may be found.
  6. A statement that you will accept service of process from the person who submitted the original infringement notice or that person's agent.

Please also provide an email address so we can contact you about your counter-notice.

When we receive a counter-notice that meets the DMCA's requirements, we promptly forward a copy to the original complainant and inform them that we will restore the material in 10 business days. We restore the material no fewer than 10 and no more than 14 business days after receiving the counter-notice, unless our designated agent first receives notice that the complainant has filed an action seeking a court order to restrain the allegedly infringing activity.

A counter-notice addresses a copyright removal. If material is also restricted for a separate reason, we will explain that reason and the applicable appeal process. For other moderation decisions, see our free-expression and appeals policy.

Repeat infringement

ByteWave's policy is to terminate, in appropriate circumstances, the accounts of users who are repeat copyright infringers. We consider the circumstances, including relevant notices, counter-notices, retractions, and court decisions, when applying this policy.

Information shared during this process

We may share copyright notices, including contact details, with the affected user to explain the claim and allow a response. We forward qualifying counter-notices, including the contact details they contain, to the original complainant. Do not include passwords or unrelated sensitive information.

Knowingly materially misrepresenting infringement, or that material was removed by mistake or misidentification, can result in liability under the DMCA.

Learn more from the U.S. Copyright Office's Section 512 guidance and 17 U.S.C. § 512.
